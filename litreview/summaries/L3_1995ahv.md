# Evidence for gluon interference in hadronic Z decays
- **ID / links:** L3:1995ahv · INSPIRE 394961 · arXiv — · DOI 10.1016/0370-2693(95)00552-V
- **Authors / collab, year, journal:** L3 Collaboration, M. Acciarri et al., 1995, Phys. Lett. B 353 (1995) 145–154 (received 11 April 1995)
- **Type:** experimental
- **Relevance:** 3 · RQs: 1a, 2, 3
- **Full text source:** zotero (journal PDF with text layer; Figs. 1–4 read from 300 dpi renderings)

Page anchors are journal pages 145–154. Physics content is on pp. 148–153. **Note for the orchestrator:** this is *not* a three-jet / inter-jet string-effect paper. It is an **inclusive two-particle angular-correlation** test of intra-jet angular ordering, with no jet finding. L3's string-effect paper is its ref. [14], L3, PLB 345 (1995) 74 (see Leads). No numbers are tabulated. All quantitative values below are read from the figures to about ±0.002 (PPCA) or ±0.005 (EECA).

## Takeaways
- L3 tests **soft-gluon coherence (angular ordering)** with no jet finding. It measures the energy-energy correlation asymmetry EECA(χ) = EEC(180° − χ) − EEC(χ) and introduces a new unweighted **particle-particle correlation asymmetry** PPCA(χ) = PPC(180° − χ) − PPC(χ). The sample is ≈ 377k hadronic Z decays (charged tracks only) at √s = 91.2 GeV, corrected to generator level (p. 150–151).
- In both variables, coherent showers (JETSET 7.3 PS with angular ordering, HERWIG 5.6, ARIADNE 4.04) describe the data "reasonably well over the full angular range". Incoherent models (JETSET PS with angular ordering off, COJETS 6.23) fail at small χ: below 54° for PPCA and below 36° for EECA (Figs. 3, 4; p. 152–153).
- Approximate size of the failure, read from Fig. 3b: in the PPCA plateau region χ ≈ 20°–45°, data ≈ −0.003 to −0.008 rad⁻¹ vs incoherent JETSET ≈ −0.010 to −0.019. The deviation is δ ≈ −0.012 to −0.014 at 15°–27°, falling to ≈ −0.002 by 45°; at 15°–39° this is several times the stat+syst band of about ±0.002–0.004. COJETS lies systematically low for χ ≳ 15° (δ ≈ −0.005 to −0.0095).
- In EECA, HERWIG is "significantly worse" than JETSET and ARIADNE: it is ≈ 0.02–0.025 too low near the peak, χ ≈ 15°–21° (≈ 0.015 at 27°) (Fig. 4a, p. 153). The JETSET 2nd-order matrix element (no shower) fits EECA but not PPCA. The authors take this as evidence that the PPCA is sensitive to multiple soft emission (p. 153).
- The discriminating power survives ±1σ variations of the JETSET tune, vector/pseudoscalar ratio changes, and switching Bose–Einstein (BE) correlations on and off (Fig. 2; p. 151–152). Coherence and hadronization are not separated explicitly: all coherent models pass, whether string (JETSET, ARIADNE) or cluster (HERWIG).

## Setup
- LEP1, L3, 1992 data at √s = 91.2 GeV: 654k hadronic Z decays (p. 150).
- **Selection** (p. 150):
  - Calorimetric preselection: 0.6 < E_vis/√s < 1.4, |E_∥|/E_vis < 0.4, E_⊥/E_vis < 0.4, N_cluster > 12 (98% hadronic efficiency), and thrust axis within 45° < θ < 135°.
  - Track selection: DCA < 20 mm, p_T > 100 MeV/c. Track-based cuts: 0.3 < Σ|p|/√s, Σ|p_∥|/Σ|p| < 0.75, |Σp_⊥|/Σ|p| < 0.75, N_ch > 4.
  - This leaves ≈ 377k events. Only central-tracker charged tracks are used, with pair angular resolution better than 0.7°. "Energies" in EEC are track momenta (p. 149–150).
- **Observables** (p. 149):
  - EEC(χ) = (1/N_ev Δχ) Σ_events Σ_{i≠j} E_iE_j/E_vis² δ_bin(χ − χ_ij).
  - PPC(χ) is the same with weight 1/N_ch².
  - Asymmetries are taken as above. Bins are 6° wide (p. 150), and the first bin (0–6°) is excluded from interpretation (p. 152).
  - Rationale (authors'): at the Z most events are 2-jet, so the χ > 90° correlations approximate what the χ < 90° intra-jet correlation would be "in the absence of angular ordering". Subtracting them removes uninteresting correlations and partly cancels hadronization, detector and MC effects. Three-jet events give large negative values at small χ. 2- and 3-jet events are deliberately not separated, to avoid jet-algorithm systematics (p. 149).
- **Models** (p. 148–150):
  - **Coherent:**
    - JETSET 7.3 PS: angular-ordering veto in the shower, then string fragmentation.
    - HERWIG 5.6: angular-ordered shower, then cluster fragmentation.
    - ARIADNE 4.04: colour-dipole cascade, then string fragmentation.
  - **Incoherent:**
    - JETSET 7.3 PS with angular ordering switched off.
    - COJETS 6.23: incoherent shower plus independent fragmentation.
  - **Fixed order:** JETSET 7.3 ME, O(α_s²) matrix element, no shower.
  - Tunes: all models are tuned by L3 (Z. Phys. C55 (1992) 39) except non-angular-ordered JETSET. With the coherent JETSET tune, that model has ⟨N_ch⟩ ≈ 0.8 too high. This was removed by shifting the tune parameters by less than 1σ (p. 150).
  - BE correlations: JETSET and ARIADNE include JETSET's Gaussian BE parametrisation (PARJ(92) = 1.5, PARJ(93) = 0.33). HERWIG and COJETS have none (footnote 7, p. 152).
- **Detector correction** (p. 150–151): based on ≈ 346k fully simulated JETSET PS (angular-ordered) events.
  - Additive per-bin correction for PPCA and EECA.
  - Multiplicative correction for PPC and EEC.
  - Cross-check with HERWIG-based corrections; the difference between the two is the dominant systematic.
  - In the most discriminating region, χ ≈ 6°–40°, the corrections are < 0.01 (PPCA) and < 0.06 (EECA).
- **Systematics** (p. 152):
  - (a) Model-parameter uncertainty: the r.m.s. of ±1σ variations of Λ_LL, σ_q and the Lund b parameter (footnote 5; shower cut-off fixed at JETSET default 1 GeV).
  - (b) Detector-correction uncertainty: the JETSET vs HERWIG difference.
  - The bands in Figs. 3 and 4 combine stat + (a) + (b).

## Results
- **Corrected PPC and EEC (Fig. 1):** the JETSET- and HERWIG-based corrections give nearly identical distributions. Statistical errors are smaller than the points.
- **Model sensitivity (Fig. 2a,c):**
  - In JETSET, coherent vs incoherent PPCA differ strongly below ≈ 54°. For example, at χ ≈ 20°–30° coherent ≈ −0.002 to −0.003 vs incoherent ≈ −0.015 to −0.019 rad⁻¹ (Fig. 2a).
  - The ±1σ tune bands are much narrower than this difference (p. 151–152).
  - EECA: the coherent peak is ≈ 0.155 vs incoherent ≈ 0.125 at χ ≈ 15°, with a narrower discriminating range (Fig. 2c).
  - Changing the vector/pseudoscalar ratio to 1/3 or 3 gives shifts comparable to the tune variations (p. 152).
- **Bose–Einstein (Fig. 2b,d):** BE lowers the PPCA at all χ for both coherent and incoherent JETSET, but leaves the shape "largely unaffected" (p. 152). For EECA, BE is small for χ > 6° (p. 152). Turning BE off in incoherent JETSET "does not raise its PPCA points enough" (Fig. 2b; p. 153). The authors argue that adding BE to COJETS would push it further from the data (p. 153).
- **PPCA vs models (Fig. 3):**
  - Data: ≈ −0.05 in the first bin, ≈ −0.035 at 9°, ≈ −0.012 at 15°, then a shallow plateau of ≈ −0.003 to −0.008 between 20° and 60°, rising to 0 at 90° (Fig. 3a).
  - Coherent JETSET, ARIADNE and HERWIG have |δ| ≲ 0.003 for χ ≳ 15°, mostly inside the band (Fig. 3a lower panel). HERWIG is +0.01 at 9°, which lies in the region where BE matters and HERWIG has no BE.
  - Incoherent JETSET: δ ≈ −0.004 at 9°, ≈ −0.012 to −0.014 at 15°–27°, ≈ −0.006 to −0.007 at 33°–39°, ≈ −0.002 at 45°, consistent with zero above ≈ 50° (Fig. 3b).
  - COJETS: δ ≈ −0.005 to −0.0095 for χ ≳ 15° (≈ +0.003 at 9°), "although the shape of its distribution is rather similar to that of the data" (p. 152–153). COJETS parameter variations did not fix it (p. 153).
  - JETSET ME: δ ≈ −0.012 to −0.013 at 3°–9°, ≈ −0.004 to −0.006 at 15°–51°, then → 0 (Fig. 3b).
- **EECA vs models (Fig. 4):**
  - Data peak ≈ 0.16 at χ ≈ 15° (Fig. 4a).
  - JETSET-coherent and ARIADNE are within the band above ≈ 20°.
  - HERWIG: δ ≈ −0.02 to −0.025 at 15°–21° and ≈ −0.015 at 27°, outside the band, "significantly worse" (p. 153).
  - Incoherent JETSET and COJETS: δ ≈ −0.033 to −0.037 (JETSET) and ≈ −0.042 to −0.049 (COJETS) at 9°–15°, back within the band by ≈ 35°–40° (Fig. 4b).
  - JETSET ME: δ ≈ −0.007 to −0.015 at 9°–27°, near or inside the band, "agrees satisfactorily" (p. 153).
- **Authors' conclusion:** "Both correlation asymmetries lead to the same conclusion: The data are generally in agreement with coherent models and strongly disfavour the incoherent models" (p. 153). No χ² or significance is quoted.

## Assessment
- **What is tested.** The observable is mainly sensitive to *intra-jet* angular ordering, the hump-backed / small-angle soft-gluon suppression (RQ 1a). It is not sensitive to the inter-jet q–q̄ depletion (RQ 1b), although 3-jet events contribute to the small-χ asymmetry. It complements rather than replaces the string-effect measurements (JADE_1981ofk, JADE_1985mmd, OPAL_1991xwi).
- **Does it separate perturbative coherence from hadronization?** Only partially.
  - The cleanest comparison is JETSET with angular ordering on vs off. The same string fragmentation and a re-tuned ⟨N_ch⟩ isolate the shower's coherence, and the difference (≈ 0.012–0.014 in PPCA at 15°–27°, ≈ 0.006 at 33°–39°) is several times the quoted band. That is good evidence that the *shower-level* angular-ordering veto is needed, *given* Lund string fragmentation.
  - COJETS fails differently (an overall offset with the right shape) and also changes the hadronization model. Its failure is therefore not a clean coherence test, and the authors themselves note COJETS's known p_T problems (p. 153).
  - HERWIG (cluster) passing PPCA shows the coherent conclusion does not depend on string hadronization.
- **Caveats.**
  - The detector correction is derived from coherent JETSET. The systematic only compares it with HERWIG, also coherent, so it does not test bias toward incoherent shapes. That bias is probably small given corrections < 0.01 in PPCA, but the paper does not test it.
  - The incoherent JETSET was not fully retuned; only the multiplicity was fixed.
  - The model-parameter systematic uses JETSET variations only.
  - No numeric tables or significance. The data are not on HEPData as far as this letter indicates (not checked).
  - The PPCA's sensitivity to BE and soft non-perturbative physics, which the authors acknowledge, is the main interpretational weakness. The BE-off check addresses it only for JETSET.
- **Tension/consistency.** The JETSET ME result (fits EECA, fails PPCA) is a useful hint that the energy-weighted EECA is dominated by hard 3-jet topology. Small-angle coherence needs the unweighted, soft-sensitive PPCA. HERWIG's EECA deficit near the peak is a model-specific shortfall and is not interpreted further.
