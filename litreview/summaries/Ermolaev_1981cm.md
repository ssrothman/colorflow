# Log-log asymptotic form of exclusive cross-sections in quantum chromodynamics (Engl. transl.: "Doubly logarithmic asymptotic behavior of exclusive cross sections in quantum chromodynamics")

## Takeaways
- Gives the double-log (DLA) exclusive cross section for e+e- → γ* → q q̄ + n soft gluons, for any n. This is one of the two original derivations of soft-gluon angular ordering (with Mueller 1981). In each contributing region, every gluon that emits further gluons does so at successively *smaller* angles relative to itself (Eq. 2). Gluons emitted from the quark (antiquark) are ordered in angle to the quark line (Eq. 3). Gluon energies are strongly ordered (Eq. 1).
- In each such region the summed-over-diagrams Born amplitude factorises into a product of eikonal factors (e_i·P_i)/(k_i·P_i). Each factor refers only to the gluon's "parent" P_i (Eq. 4). The colour/spin-summed cross section is a product of independent emissions with Casimir C_F (emitter is a quark) or C_V = N (emitter is a gluon), each with the soft-collinear weight P_i² θ²/(k_i·P_i)² (Eq. 7). This is the coherent branching (tree) structure.
- The authors stress that this form comes only after *summing* Feynman diagrams: "a gauge in which only one diagram contributes does not exist" (p. 271). Angular ordering is therefore an interference (coherence) result, not a property of single diagrams.
- Virtual corrections enter as a DL Sudakov-type form factor F (Eq. 5). For each gluon the angular integral is cut at its emission angle θ_i, while for the q and q̄ it runs to angle 1. Real and virtual pieces together have a classical/probabilistic ("decay probability", parton-language) interpretation (p. 271–272). The DLs cancel when summed over n = 0…∞ (p. 272).
- The paper is a short letter and states its results without derivation: "the analysis … is rather complicated and cumbersome, we shall skip it here" (p. 270).
## Setup
- Process: single-photon e+e- annihilation into q q̄ + n gluons at Born level, then with DL virtual corrections (p. 269–271). Only diagrams with quark–gluon and three-gluon vertices; four-gluon vertices excluded (p. 270). g → q q̄ splitting is not considered. Physical gluon polarisation vectors; angle and energy variables used instead of Sudakov variables (p. 270).
- Approximation: double-logarithmic. Only regions with strongly ordered soft gluon energies ω_n ≪ ω_{n−1} ≪ … ≪ ω_1 ≪ ε_± ≈ ε contribute (Eq. 1, p. 270), where ε_± are the q̄/q energies in the c.m. frame.
- Assignment of parents (p. 270): in a given Feynman diagram, each virtual line carries approximately the energy of its most energetic final-state descendant. The line of gluon i is the virtual line with energy ≈ ω_i. If it starts on gluon line j (possible only for i > j), "gluon j emits gluon i"; if it starts on the quark (antiquark) line, gluon i is emitted by the quark (antiquark).
- Each diagram is paired with a kinematic region defined by angles. If gluon i, emitted by j, successively emits i_1, i_2, …, i_r along its line, then θ(k_i,k_j) ≫ θ(k_i,k_{i1}) ≫ θ(k_i,k_{i2}) ≫ … ≫ θ(k_i,k_{ir}) (Eq. 2). For emission off the quark or antiquark, k_j → p_∓ (p. 270). Gluons j_1…j_m emitted successively by a quark (antiquark): 1 ≫ θ(p_∓,k_{j1}) ≫ … ≫ θ(p_∓,k_{jm}) (Eq. 3). [Eq. 2 is printed with "k_k k_{ir}" as its last term, which is evidently a typo for k_i k_{ir}. The text says e_i is the polarisation of "the jth gluon", evidently meaning the ith.]
- The authors state that the regions do not overlap ("a set of nonoverlapping regions", p. 270) and that "the sum of all these regions is equal to the total cross section" (p. 271).
## Results
- Born amplitude in a region (Eq. 4): M_0 g^n (−1)^m ∏_{i=1}^n (e_i·P_i)/(k_i·P_i) · G. Here M_0 is the colour-stripped q q̄ amplitude, m is the number of gluons emitted by the antiquark, and P_i is the 4-momentum of gluon i's emitter. G is the colour factor of the paired diagram, with λ^a/2 at quark vertices and i f^{abc} at three-gluon vertices (a = lowest-energy, b = highest-energy gluon) (p. 271).
- DL form factor (Eq. 5): F = exp[−½ w^F_{p−}(1) − ½ w^F_{p+}(1) − ½ Σ_{i=1}^n w^V_{k_i}(θ_i)]. θ_i is the angle between gluon i and its emitter. F for the no-gluon case is taken from Ref. 3 (Carazzone–Poggio–Quinn; Cornwall–Tiktopoulos) (p. 271).
- One-emission probability into a cone (Eq. 6): w_p^{F(V)}(θ) = g²/(2π)³ C_{F(V)} ∫ d³k/(2ω) · p²θ_k²/(k·p)². The angular integral runs to θ and the energy integral to p⁰ (p. 271). In the small-angle limit this is the usual dω/ω dθ²/θ² double log. Our reading: since F's quark terms have θ = 1, F is the probability of no emission at larger angles than the emitter's own production angle, which is the virtual counterpart of angular ordering.
- Spin- and colour-summed cross section per region (Eq. 7): σ_0 ∫ F² ∏_{i=1}^n g²/(2π)³ C_i d³k_i/(2ω_i) · P_i² θ²_{k_i}/(k_i·P_i)². C_i = C_F = (N²−1)/2N = 4/3 if the emitter is a quark and C_i = C_V = N = 3 if it is a gluon. The integration domain is limited by Eqs. 1–3 (p. 271).
- Interpretation (p. 271–272): "the emission of gluons in each region is essentially a classical emission in the doubly logarithmic approximation". Eq. 7 "allows an interpretation in terms of decay probabilities, which allows us to use the 'parton' language" (Ref. 4, Lipatov 1974).
- Self-consistency (p. 272): summing the total cross section over n from 0 to ∞ cancels the double logarithms. The integrals in Eqs. 6–7 must be regularised in specific calculations (p. 272).
- Motivation (p. 269–270): the more exclusive the description, the more DLs matter. g → gg decay violates the Poisson distribution of radiation that holds in QED (Ref. 2, Kuraev–Fadin 1978). Brute-force matrix elements are already "enormous" for n = 3. The authors claim the result is "surprisingly simple and physically obvious" and generalises to other processes (p. 270).
- No numerical results, multiplicity predictions or data comparison.
## Assessment
- This is the original statement of the coherent-branching / angular-ordered structure for soft gluons in DLA, with colour factors C_F and C_A per emitter. It is the formal basis of angular-ordered showers (Marchesini–Webber, HERWIG) and of the MLLA programme (cf. Dokshitzer_1987nm, Webber_1983if). It complements Mueller 1981 (Mueller:1981ex, queued), which derived the coherence-suppressed multiplicity growth. Fadin:1983aw (queued) appears to be the extended follow-up.
- No proof is given, so the claims cannot be checked from this paper. Two points are asserted rather than shown: that the regions in Eqs. 1–3 are non-overlapping and exhaustive, and that the colour factor of each region squares to a product of Casimirs.
- Scope limitations: (i) strict DLA with strong energy *and* angle ordering, so no single-log terms (hard-collinear splittings, running coupling not discussed); (ii) gluon emission only, no g → q q̄; (iii) the colour-summed cross section Eq. 7 has only Casimir factors, so colour-correlation (non-Casimir, sub-leading-N_c) interference between emitters is absent at this accuracy. That interference is the inter-jet coherence later seen in the string/drag effect and the q q̄ g antenna patterns (Azimov–Dokshitzer–Khoze–Troyan). This paper covers intra-jet coherence (RQ 1a) in DLA but does not by itself give the inter-jet radiation pattern for multi-jet events (RQ 1b).
- The probabilistic interpretation (Eq. 7 × F²) requires the regularisation the authors mention (p. 272). The infrared cutoff is not specified.
- Scan quality is good. Two typographical slips noted under Setup; neither affects the content.
