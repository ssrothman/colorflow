# Comparison of Three Jet Events With QCD Shower Models
- **ID / links:** JADE:1985mmd · INSPIRE 214360 · arXiv — · DOI 10.1016/0370-2693(85)90677-X
- **Authors / collab, year, journal:** JADE Collaboration, W. Bartel et al., 1985, Phys. Lett. B 157 (1985) 340–344 (received 29 April 1985)
- **Type:** experimental
- **Relevance:** 3 · RQs: 1b, 2, 3
- **Full text source:** zotero (journal scan with text layer; figures read from the page images, Fig. 3 also from a 300 dpi rendering)

Page anchors (p. N) are journal pages 340–344. The paper has no tables. All numbers for the ratio r are **read off Fig. 3** (bars and error bars, no printed values), to about ±0.01–0.02.

## Takeaways
- The first experimental paper to show that the **string effect** (fewer particles between the two quark jets than next to the gluon jet in e⁺e⁻ → 3 jets) is also reproduced by a **parton shower with soft-gluon interference (angular ordering)**, Webber's 1984 cluster model, and not only by Lund string fragmentation. Showers ordered in virtuality (Gottschalk) and independent-fragmentation models (Hoyer, Ali) fail (Fig. 3, p. 343).
- String-effect ratio r = N(#1–#3)/N(#1–#2) in the central 0.3 < θ/θ_ik < 0.7 of each inter-jet sector (jet #3 = gluon candidate). Values read from Fig. 3: data ≈ 1.39 ± 0.04 (all particles), ≈ 1.73 ± 0.13 (p_out > 0.3 GeV/c), ≈ 1.9 ± 0.2 (kaon-enriched). Lund 2nd-order ≈ 1.33–1.39 / 1.46–1.60 / 1.55–1.85. Webber ≈ 1.44–1.52 / 1.66–1.86 / 1.61–2.00. Gottschalk, Ali and Hoyer all lie between ≈ 0.9 and 1.2 in every sample (Fig. 3).
- The same pattern holds for the in-plane energy and particle flow (Fig. 2a–c) and for the asymmetric in-plane p_T of particles inside the quark jets, ⟨p_⊥^in⟩ vs p_∥ (Fig. 4). Webber and Lund describe the data; Gottschalk, Ali and Hoyer do not (p. 344).
- **Parton-level evidence that the effect is perturbative:** at the end of the cascade, before cluster decay, Webber and Gottschalk partons already differ in the same way as the hadrons (Fig. 2d,e). The authors therefore attribute the difference "not … [to the] treatment of the final decay of the clusters into hadrons, but [to] the difference in the QCD calculations" (p. 342–343). The paper does not say "LPHD", but this is an early form of that argument.
- The Webber model does **not** describe the inclusive energy-energy correlation (EEC) well. It undershoots the 3-jet rate, which the authors attribute to the missing full 3-parton matrix element (Fig. 1, p. 342). The 3-jet comparisons are therefore normalised to the same number of 3-jet events (p. 342).

## Setup
- PETRA, JADE detector. The EEC comparison is at √s = 34 GeV (Fig. 1). The √s and statistics of the 3-jet sample are **not stated** in this letter. Planar 3-jet selection criteria are "given in refs. [5,6]" (JADE PLB 134B (1984) 275; Z. Phys. C21 (1983) 37) (footnote 3, p. 342).
- **Jet ordering / gluon identification:** jets are projected onto the event plane. Jet #1 lies opposite the smallest inter-jet angle and jet #3 opposite the largest. In a q q̄ g interpretation, #1 and #2 are "the fragments of quarks in the majority of the cases" (p. 342). No tagging purity is quoted here. For the 1981 sample it was ≈ 50% gluon-in-jet-#3 (see JADE_1981ofk).
- **Observables:**
  1. EEC and its asymmetry (AEEC), all multihadronic events (Fig. 1).
  2. In-plane energy flow (1/E) dE/dθ and particle flow (1/n) dn/dθ vs angle θ, running #1 → #2 → #3 → #1 (Fig. 2a,b). The same particle flow is also shown for p_out > 0.3 GeV/c, where p_out is the momentum normal to the event plane (Fig. 2c).
  3. r: the ratio of particle counts between jets #1–#3 and #1–#2, restricted to 0.3 < θ_ij/θ_ik < 0.7 (Fig. 3 axis label). It is given for all particles, for p_out > 0.3 GeV/c, and for a ≈ 50% kaon sample (p. 343).
  4. ⟨p_⊥^in⟩ vs p_∥ for jets #1 and #2, where the sign of p_⊥^in is defined relative to jet #3 (Fig. 4 insert; method from ref. [6]).
- **Models compared:**
  - Lund string with the 2nd-order matrix element ("Lund 2.O.").
  - Independent fragmentation: Ali et al. (PLB 93B (1980) 155) and Hoyer et al. "g = q" (NPB B161 (1979) 349).
  - Gottschalk QCD shower (NPB B214 (1983) 201). Virtuality-ordered leading-log cascade ending in colour-singlet clusters; no angular ordering (p. 341).
  - Webber shower (NPB B238 (1984) 492). Evolution like Gottschalk's but with **angular ordering** implementing soft-gluon interference (refs. [13]: Mueller; Bassetto–Ciafaloni–Marchesini–Mueller; Dokshitzer–Fadin–Khoze), followed by cluster decay. Parameters: Λ_QCD = 250 MeV, Q₀² = 0.36 GeV² (printed "GeV"), M_f = 4 GeV, u,d,s quark masses 0.3, 0.3, 0.5 GeV (footnote 2, p. 342).
- **Corrections:** the letter does not say whether the data are corrected for detector effects or whether the MC passed through detector simulation. Data error bars appear statistical, but this is not stated. The MC bars in Fig. 3 have finite heights, presumably MC statistics, also not stated.

## Results
- **EEC/AEEC (Fig. 1, √s = 34 GeV):** Lund describes both EEC and AEEC. Webber is close to the EEC (lower panel) but falls clearly below the AEEC for θ ≳ 30° (upper panel). Read from Fig. 1, A(θ)·sinθ at θ ≈ 45° is ≈ 0.033 in data vs ≈ 0.022 for Webber, while the peak near 25° is only slightly low (≈ 0.047 vs ≈ 0.050). The authors attribute this to too few 3-jet events in the shower model (p. 342).
- **Particle/energy flow (Fig. 2a–c):** Webber reproduces the data. Gottschalk "predicts too much energy and too many particles in the region between the jets", most visibly in the #1–#2 (q q̄) valley near θ ≈ 60°–80° (Fig. 2a,b). In data the #1–#2 depletion grows when p_out > 0.3 GeV/c is required (Fig. 2b vs 2c). Gottschalk shows this trend only weakly. "It only occurs in the Webber model with the strength required by the data" (p. 342).
- **Partons (Fig. 2d,e):** the model-only energy and particle flows of the final partons show the same Webber–Gottschalk differences as the hadron level: Webber has a deeper #1–#2 valley than Gottschalk (p. 342–343).
- **String-effect ratio r (Fig. 3; values read from the figure):**

  | sample | data | Lund 2.O. | Webber | Gottschalk | Ali | Hoyer g=q |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | all particles | 1.39 ± 0.04 | 1.33–1.39 | 1.44–1.52 | 1.01–1.07 | 1.04–1.10 | 1.00–1.05 |
  | p_out > 0.3 GeV/c | 1.73 ± 0.13 | 1.46–1.60 | 1.66–1.86 | 0.98–1.14 | 1.06–1.22 | 0.89–1.03 |
  | kaon-enriched | 1.90 ± 0.20 | 1.55–1.85 | 1.61–2.00 | 0.90–1.10 | 1.00–1.20 | 0.90–1.10 |

  The authors' summary: "Only the Lund and the Webber models provide a reasonable description of the data, whereas the independent fragmentation models and the Gottschalk model predict values for r close to unity" (p. 343). For all particles the data lie ≈ 10σ (statistical) above r = 1 (our arithmetic from the Fig. 3 reading: 0.39/0.04). Webber is ≈ 0.05–0.13 above the data (≈ 1–3 data σ) and Lund is at or just below the data (within ≈ 1σ). The paper quotes no χ² or significance.
- **Intra-jet asymmetry (Fig. 4):** for p_∥ ≳ 3 GeV/c, particles in jets #1 and #2 lean away from jet #3 in the data. ⟨p_⊥^in⟩ reaches ≈ +0.1 GeV/c for jet #1 at p_∥ ≈ 6 GeV/c and ≈ −0.1 to −0.16 GeV/c for jet #2 at p_∥ ≈ 4.5–5.7 GeV/c (sign convention as in the insert; read from Fig. 4). Lund and Webber follow this trend. Gottschalk and Ali stay near zero or have the opposite sign (Fig. 4, p. 344).
- **Gluon-jet broadening:** Lund and Webber predict quark/gluon width differences in "rough agreement" with data, and Gottschalk a smaller effect. No figure; details deferred to A. Petersen, DESY F22-84-02 (p. 344).
- **Authors' conclusion:** "experimental distributions which are particularly sensitive to the string effect, are also reproduced by QCD shower models in which soft gluon interference effects are taken into account" (p. 344).

## Assessment
- This is the experimental counterpart of the Azimov–Dokshitzer–Khoze–Troyan drag/coherence explanation (PLB 165 (1985) 147, published later the same year; not cited here). It establishes that the string effect, until then taken as evidence for string over independent fragmentation (cf. JADE_1981ofk), **does not by itself discriminate string hadronization from perturbative coherence**. Both Lund and an angular-ordered shower with cluster hadronization fit, and the result itself does not choose between them. The clean discriminant is coherent vs incoherent (virtuality-ordered) evolution. Independent fragmentation and the Gottschalk shower are both excluded at a level that looks unambiguous by eye, with r ≈ 1 against ≈ 1.4 in data, though no formal statistics are given.
- The parton-level plot (Fig. 2d,e) supports a perturbative origin only *within the Webber model*: angular ordering of the final partons already produces the depletion. The paper does not test whether the cluster stage alone could generate or wash out the effect, for example Webber partons with a different hadronizer. The claim is therefore model-internal.
- Why Webber's angular ordering produces an *inter-jet* asymmetry: in the Marchesini–Webber scheme each parton's initial emission cone is bounded by its colour-connected partner (see Marchesini_1987cf). So the q and q̄ cones open toward the gluon and not toward each other. This mechanism is our understanding from later literature and is **not stated in this letter**, which mentions only "suppression of the large angle emission of soft gluons" (p. 341).
- Caveats: no statement on detector correction or on how the MC was compared, the selection is only by reference, there is no tagging purity, and the numbers must be read from figures. Model tunes are not stated except for Webber. Webber's poor AEEC shows the model is not globally tuned or matched to the matrix element. Webber's slight overshoot of r for all particles would therefore be hard to interpret.
- In the p_out-cut sample Webber (1.66–1.86) brackets the data (1.73 ± 0.13) and Lund (1.46–1.60) slightly undershoots, by ≈ 1–2σ. It is consistent with later LEP findings that both coherent-shower families reproduce the string effect (OPAL_1991xwi: Jetset 1.67 vs data 1.62 ± 0.07).

## Leads
- **Azimov, Dokshitzer, Khoze, Troyan, "The String Effect and QCD Coherence", PLB 165 (1985) 147** (Azimov:1985zta, already queued with expect 3). The perturbative drag explanation that this experimental result anticipates.
- **B.R. Webber, NPB 238 (1984) 492.** The coherent shower model used here. Defines how angular ordering and colour connection set the cones (RQ2). Check whether it is in the DB.
- **T.D. Gottschalk, NPB 214 (1983) 201.** The incoherent (virtuality-ordered) cluster shower that fails. A useful "no-coherence" baseline for RQ2 history.
- **JADE, PLB 134B (1984) 275** (JADE:1983mvo, queued) and **JADE, Z. Phys. C21 (1983) 37** (JADE:1983ihf, queued). These hold the 3-jet selection and earlier string-effect and p_⊥^in results. The selection details are needed to interpret this letter.
- **JADE, Z. Phys. C25 (1984) 231.** The EEC/AEEC data in Fig. 1 come from here: string vs IF in all multihadronic events.
- **TPC, H. Aihara et al., PRL 54 (1985) 270** (likely TPCTwoGamma:1984tkq or :1984sji, queued). Independent PEP confirmation of the string effect.
- **MAC, E. Fernandez et al., SLAC-PUB-3385 (Phys. Rev. D 1985).** EEC confirmation of the string-model preference.
- **A. Petersen, DESY F22-84-02 (1984), thesis.** Gluon-jet broadening comparison with Lund/Webber/Gottschalk. Low priority, probably hard to obtain.
- **Chudakov, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Fiz. 19 (1955) 650.** The QED analogue of coherence (historical context only, low priority).
