## Takeaways
- The canonical statement of the **Lund string picture of the qq̄g event**: the colour field is stretched q → g → q̄, the gluon is a kink (transverse excitation) on a single triplet string, with no new parameters (pp. 83–85, Fig. 4.1). The two string pieces move transversely with v = cos(θ/2) and fragment in their own rest frames; boosted back, hadrons lie along two hyperbolae in momentum space, ~300 MeV from the origin (p. 90, Figs. 4.8–4.9). This gives fewer particles between q and q̄ than in the q–g and g–q̄ regions: the **string effect**.
- Updated JADE comparison (Tab. 4.1, from [50] = JADE PLB 101B (1981) 129 + A. Petersen, Moriond 1983): ratio of particle numbers in the central angular range 0.3 ≤ θ/θ_ik ≤ 0.7 between jets #3–#1 vs #1–#2: data 1.39 ± 0.04, Lund 1.33 ± 0.03, Hoyer independent fragmentation 1.09 ± 0.03 (g ≠ q) / 1.03 ± 0.03 (g = q). The ratio grows for p_T^out > 0.4 GeV (data 1.8 ± 0.2) and kaons (1.9 ± 0.2), and is larger in energy flow (1.56 ± 0.04), as the Lorentz-boost origin predicts (Tab. 4.1, p. 92; discussion p. 91).
- Alternative colour-field topology: **Montvay junction** with an octet string from the gluon (tension κ_g) joining two triplet strings (Fig. 4.2). It interpolates between the string and independent-fragmentation event shapes, collapsing to the Lund kink for κ_g ≥ 2κ. The authors say the data favour the Lund case or a large κ_g (p. 84, p. 93).
- The colour-flow assumption feeds directly into **"hadronization-independent" α_s determinations**. In the Lund model, fragmentation reduces the energy–energy-correlation asymmetry ΔΣ by ~a factor 2, against 1.1–1.2 for independent jets (p. 105). With Lund, CELLO needs α_s ≈ 0.25 instead of 0.17 (p. 104, Fig. 4.20), and Lund 3-jet events "look more two-jetlike" (Figs. 4.22–4.23, p. 105–107).
- §6 is an early explicit statement of the **colour-topology ambiguity** behind later colour reconnection. In e⁺e⁻ → qq̄gg the field can be stretched in two ways (Fig. 6.1). In gg → gg with octet remnants, four octets connect in six ways (Fig. 6.3). Separating amplitudes by colour configuration is unique only for N_c → ∞ (p. 141). Proposed probes: K K̄ correlations for the field orientation (p. 140), low-momentum particles and correlations in high-p⊥ events, and prompt-photon events (p. 142, Fig. 6.5).

## Setup
- Review of the Lund string fragmentation model (the basis of the Lund JETSET/LEPTO MCs, refs [13], [57], [58], [59]) and its comparisons with PETRA e⁺e⁻ (JADE, CELLO, TASSO; W ≈ 30–34 GeV) and EMC/BEBC/ABCDLOS μp/νp DIS data (280 GeV μp, p. 110). Only first-order QCD matrix elements (Eq. 4.8) plus soft/collinear-gluon corrections; no parton shower.
- Comparison models: the independent-fragmentation (IF) Monte Carlos of Hoyer et al. [45] (NPB 161 (1979) 349) and Ali et al. [46] (PLB 93B (1980) 155). These are Field–Feynman jets joined at the CM origin, not Lorentz invariant, with energy/momentum conserved "post facto" (p. 83). The Hoyer model is run in two variants, g ≠ q (softer gluon fragmentation, larger ⟨p⊥⟩) and g = q (p. 91; Tab. 4.1, p. 92).
- 3-jet/2-jet separation uses invariant-mass cuts M_qg, M_q̄g ≥ 4 GeV and M_qg M_q̄g / M_qq̄ ≥ 2.8 GeV (Eq. 4.11, p. 94). The authors stress that the Lund picture is infrared stable (soft/collinear gluon → smooth transition to a 2-jet event, Figs. 4.13–4.14) while IF is not explicitly so (p. 94).
- Gluon kink dynamics: the retarding force on the kink is 2κ, against κ on a quark (κ ≈ 1 GeV/fm ≈ 0.2 GeV²). The authors compare this with the QCD Casimir force ratio 2/(1−1/N²) = 9/4 (p. 85).

## Results
- **Gluon fragmentation function** in the kink model: z D_gluon(z) = 2 + 4z + 4z² (z ≤ ½), 4z(1−z) (z ≥ ½) (Eq. 4.7, p. 88). For z → 0 this is twice D_quark (= 1/z), with an extra (1−z) at z → 1 (p. 88). π/K/p spectra are in Fig. 4.6. The leading particle in a gluon jet has roughly twice the probability to be a baryon, because the field breaks twice (p. 89, Fig. 4.7).
- **String effect (Figs. 4.10–4.11, Tab. 4.1, pp. 91–92).** Planar 3-jet JADE events, with jets ordered by decreasing energy (#3 is usually the gluon). The data show more particles between the softest jet and the two harder jets, and fewer between #1 and #2. Lund (string via the gluon) describes this; an IF model joined at the origin "cannot reproduce this experimental fact equally well", even with quark and gluon fragmentation parameters fitted to the in-jet flow (p. 91). The authors note that the "true" Lund effect is much larger than what is visible, because the gluon jet is often misidentified (p. 91). Tab. 4.1 (statistical errors only):

  | | Data | Lund | Hoyer g≠q | Hoyer g=q |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | N, all | 1.39 ± 0.04 | 1.33 ± 0.03 | 1.09 ± 0.03 | 1.03 ± 0.03 |
  | N, p_T^out < 0.2 GeV | 1.36 ± 0.05 | 1.27 ± 0.04 | 1.09 ± 0.04 | 1.05 ± 0.04 |
  | N, p_T^out > 0.4 GeV | 1.8 ± 0.2 | 1.61 ± 0.12 | 1.18 ± 0.12 | 1.12 ± 0.11 |

  For "all", data − Hoyer(g≠q) = 0.30 ± 0.05 (≈ 6σ, our arithmetic with statistical errors combined in quadrature). Data − Lund = 0.06 ± 0.05.
- **Gluon-jet ⟨p⊥⟩ (Fig. 4.12, p. 92):** JADE [51] (DESY 82-086) finds ⟨p⊥⟩ in the lowest-energy jet somewhat larger than in jets #1, #2. The string model reproduces this (Fig. 4.12d). IF would need gluon fragmentation p⊥ ~50% higher than quark p⊥ (Fig. 4.12c, p. 92).
- **Junction alternative (pp. 84, 92–93, 105):** a junction near the gluon gives Lund; a junction near the CM origin gives an IF-like structure. The junction is at rest at the origin only when the three string forces balance (p. 93). ΔΣ reduction factor: ≈ 1.4 for κ_g = κ and ≈ 1.65 for κ_g = 1.5κ, against ≈ 2 for Lund and 1.1–1.2 for IF, for π/5 < θ < 2π/5 (p. 105). An octet field could break by gluon-pair production (glueballs, more η′/ω/φ; Peterson–Walsh [48]) (p. 85).
- **α_s and colour flow (pp. 104–107):** CELLO [61] needs α_s ≈ 0.25 with Lund against ≈ 0.17 with Hoyer at W ≈ 33 GeV (p. 104, Fig. 4.20). In Lund, low-momentum particles fill the q–g and g–q̄ regions asymmetrically (Fig. 4.21). The ratio of 3-jet counts (IF/Lund) at x_min = 0 is ≈ 1.2 for sphericity and ≈ 1.5 for linear sphericity, approaching 1 as soft particles are cut (Fig. 4.23, value read from figure). Because the Lund gluon jet is softer than the Hoyer/Ali gluon jets, this effect alone may typically require α_s 20–30% higher in Lund for the same number of 3-jet events (p. 107). JADE [65] finds similar α_s in different models for suitably defined cluster thrusts (p. 107). The authors' conclusion is that the hope of studying perturbative QCD and fragmentation separately "is lost" at these energies (pp. 107–108).
- **Soft gluons (§4.5, pp. 94–102):** a soft gluon disturbs hadrons within |y − y_g| ≲ 1, falling like exp(−|y − y_g|) (p. 95; Eq. 4.12 for the 1/cosh(y − y_g) profile). Gluons emitted more than ~1 unit apart "will not interfere" (p. 95). This is a string-model locality statement, not perturbative coherence. Soft gluons summed per unit rapidity into effective gluons (Eqs. 4.29–4.33) raise the central-hadron Gaussian p⊥ width from ≈ 0.35 to ≈ 0.42 GeV/c, with ≈ 800 MeV/c total recoil at PETRA (p. 101). Collinear gluons soften z spectra as (1 − z)^β with β ≈ 0.3–0.5 (Eqs. 4.21–4.22, p. 97).
- **DIS (§4.6.2, pp. 108–116):** in boson–gluon fusion (qq̄) events the octet target remnant is split into a diquark (2/3 of the energy) and a quark, forming two singlet strings (p. 109). This is a specific colour-flow choice. EMC p⊥-balance data favour soft-gluon p⊥ over a large primordial k⊥ (Fig. 4.32, p. 113). The parton-level QCD forward–backward energy-flow asymmetry (Eq. 4.40) is washed out by hadronization at 280 GeV μp. The residual hadron-level asymmetry comes mostly from baryon production in the target region (Figs. 4.33–4.34, pp. 114–116). Hadron-level IF and Lund give the same energy-flow curves (Fig. 4.33 caption).
- **Onium → ggg (p. 116, Fig. 4.35):** a closed triangular string with three kinks. With the usual cuts all Υ 3g decays reduce to gg, "in disagreement with experimental data"; the authors say this is unproblematic for toponium.
- **Baryon junction (§5.3.2, pp. 128–129, Figs. 5.15–5.16):** the initial baryon is a Y-shaped string with a junction that carries no energy-momentum. When one valence quark is struck, the junction moves at constant velocity, and one remaining quark always ends up in the same hadron as the junction, forming the leading baryon (p. 129). Baryons in fragmentation come from diquark–antidiquark pair production, P(qq)/P(q) = 0.085 fitted to TASSO at 34 GeV (p. 80).
- **Colour topology, §6 (pp. 140–142):** the field is not fixed by the charge positions, so topology must be specified. The ambiguity in qq̄gg (Figs. 6.1–6.2) and the six gg → gg + remnant topologies (Fig. 6.3) are as listed in the Takeaways; perturbative amplitudes can be assigned to configurations [105], uniquely only for N_c → ∞ (p. 141). The fast hadrons are insensitive to the connection, so whole-event descriptions are needed [106] (p. 141). A flux-tube modification (Fig. 6.4) would give a smooth low/high-p⊥ transition. For prompt photons in pp at √s = 63 GeV (p_T^trig > 3 GeV/c) [107], the colour-field stretching gives a forward–backward asymmetry of the associated hadrons. A smaller asymmetry at lower p_⊥,in would indicate the Fig. 6.4b configuration (p. 142, Fig. 6.5). The text gives the photon direction as θ_γ ~ 20°, the caption as 155°–165°; this is presumably the same configuration measured from the other beam (unclear).
- In §2 the authors use the term "colour coherence region" for the space-time area tied to each hadron in the area law (p. 62, Figs. 2.22–2.23). This is unrelated to perturbative soft-gluon coherence.

## Assessment
- This is the standard reference for the Lund model of colour flow in qq̄g events. It contains no new measurement. The JADE numbers in Tab. 4.1 are updated relative to JADE:1981ofk (1.39 ± 0.04 against 1.35 ± 0.09), presumably from more statistics (Moriond 1983). Jet ordering is by energy here and by opposite angles in the 1981 paper, which is equivalent for massless 3-body kinematics. The underlying analysis is not documented here, and the errors are statistical only.
- The case against IF is against Hoyer-type IF only. The authors themselves show that observables designed to be hadronization-independent are not (pp. 104–107). They do not consider the perturbative (soft-gluon antenna / angular-ordering) origin of the same asymmetry, which appeared shortly after (Azimov–Dokshitzer–Khoze–Troyan 1985). Read the string effect here as "string vs Hoyer-IF", not "string vs coherent parton shower".
- The soft-gluon section treats emission as Poissonian with a cut-off and argues locality (≲ 1 unit of rapidity) from string dynamics. Angular ordering and colour coherence are absent, and DDT [55] and Parisi–Petronzio [54] are used only for p⊥ resummation (p. 99). This is an important contrast for RQ1a/2.
- §6 matters for RQ2/RQ4 historically: multiple colour-field topologies, their 1/N_c-suppressed ambiguity, and measurable consequences (K K̄ correlations, prompt-γ asymmetries) are stated here before the colour-reconnection literature. The section is explicitly preliminary ("investigations ... have just started", p. 36), with no data comparison.
- The Montvay-junction discussion, the junction baryon model, and the octet-string alternative are early sources for junction/baryon colour topologies. They are relevant to later junction CR and baryon enhancement in small systems, but are not tested quantitatively here.
- Model-dependence caveats: first-order matrix elements only; tuning differs between Lund and Hoyer, and tuning bias is not discussed. The α_s shift (0.17 → 0.25) shows that the colour-flow assumption can dominate a "QCD measurement".
