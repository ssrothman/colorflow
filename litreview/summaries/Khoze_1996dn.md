## Takeaways
- Standard reference for the "analytical perturbative approach" (APA = MLLA parton cascade + LPHD). Coherence is described as a property of soft-gluon emission: intrajet coherence gives angular ordering (AO) and the hump-backed plateau. Interjet coherence gives the string/drag effect, a classical-radiophysics antenna pattern set by the colour topology of the hard partons (Sec. 1 p. 8; Sec. 6.1).
- Key interjet result: the qq̄g antenna pattern (Eq. 6.1). Fixed-ratio predictions for symmetric (Mercedes) events: interquark-valley flow in qq̄g/qq̄γ ≈ 0.44 differential, 0.60 projected (Eqs. 6.6–6.7); qg/qq̄ valley ≈ 3.14 / 2.46 (Eqs. 6.8–6.9). The authors present DELPHI as quantitative confirmation, though with interval-specific predictions rather than the Mercedes numbers above: R_γ = 0.58 ± 0.06 vs 0.61 for the [35°, 115°] interval (Eqs. 7.2–7.3); R_g = 2.23 ± 0.37 vs ≈ 2.4 from the full MLLA flow of Ref. [147] with normalisation adjusted (asymptotic Eq. 6.9: 2.46) (Eqs. 7.4–7.5). OPAL's R₁₂ = 0.51 ± 0.03 after purity correction (p. 100) is not compared numerically with Eqs. 6.6–6.9; OPAL's fit to Eqs. 6.4–6.5 is only qualitative (p. 101).
- At leading N_C the perturbative antenna pattern and the Lund string give the same picture: two boosted qq̄ dipoles, "peaceful coexistence" (p. 83; Sec. 6.2.2 pp. 86–87). Colour-suppressed O(1/N_C²) "negative antenna" terms break the probabilistic and string picture, and MC generators omit them. The authors say these terms had **not** been seen experimentally as of 1996 (p. 83). They tie them to W⁺W⁻ interconnection (Sjöstrand–Khoze, Gustafson–Häkkinen) and to rapidity-gap events (p. 83).
- Proposed discriminating tests (RQ4): m_h / p_out dependence of the drag ratio, which should vanish asymptotically in pQCD but persist in a boosted string (pp. 87, 100); the energy dependence ΔE = C₁ + C₂α_sW (Eq. 6.12); double-inclusive interjet correlations (Eq. 6.13); azimuthal asymmetry of quark jets controlled by 1/N_C² terms (Eqs. 6.46–6.50): the colour-suppressed term dominates when q and q̄ are quasi-collinear (Θ₊₋ ≲ 37°), and in ISR events with a soft gluon orthogonal to the qq̄γ plane the asymmetry is at most ≈2 %, with sign opposite to the colour-favoured (string) term; the tagged-q-jet asymmetry in pp̄ (p. 99); out-of-plane ratios (Eqs. 6.10–6.11); the 10-item outlook list (pp. 128–129).
- LPHD is called the "Achilles heel": it is not derivable from QCD (p. 19). Its successes do not prove soft-gluon coherence in hadronization models, since an incoherent shower plus retuned string fragmentation fits the ξ spectrum equally well (p. 42).

## Setup
- Framework: soft-gluon bremsstrahlung (Eq. 2.1); formation-time argument for perturbativity, k_⊥ > R⁻¹ ~ few hundred MeV (Eqs. 2.4–2.6); generating functionals with the MLLA Master Equation (Eqs. 2.22, 2.31) and DLA (Eq. 2.36). The exact angular kernel V (Eq. 2.12) averages over azimuth to strict AO (Eq. 2.14). Beyond MLLA no probabilistic picture exists: "colour monsters" ~1/N_C² first appear at 4th order (p. 13, footnote 3). Interjet collective phenomena are probabilistic only at large N_C (fn. 3).
- LPHD (Sec. 2.3, Eq. 2.37): hadronic observables ∝ partonic ones with Q₀ ≈ Λ ≈ 250 MeV; parameters are Λ, Q₀ and normalisation K^h (pp. 8, 19, 126).
- Interjet formalism: antenna ⟨ij⟩ = a_ij/(a_i a_j) (Eq. 6.2); qq̄g pattern (Eq. 6.1), qq̄γ pattern (Eq. 6.3), event-plane projections (Eqs. 6.4–6.5). Full MLLA flow including intrajet cascades (Eqs. 6.23–6.32). Topology dependence of the 3-jet multiplicity (Eqs. 6.33–6.45). Inclusive "E^αM^βC" energy–multiplicity correlations as jet-algorithm-free observables (Sec. 6.3.1, Eqs. 6.14–6.16; E³MC for qq̄g, Eq. 6.21).
- Data surveyed: PETRA/PEP (JADE drag), LEP-1 (OPAL, L3, DELPHI, ALEPH qq̄g vs qq̄γ and Z→3 jets), Tevatron (CDF/D0 3-jet coherence, D0 W+jet, CDF restricted-cone spectra), HERA (Breit-frame spectra). Other chapters, only skimmed: multiplicity distributions/KNO (Sec. 4), intrajet correlations (Sec. 5), heavy quarks/dead cone (Sec. 9).

## Results
**Physical origin of coherence (RQ1a,b).**
- Intrajet: AO in sequential branching (Eq. 2.11) suppresses soft multiplication and gives the hump-backed ξ = ln 1/x_p spectrum. Energy of the most active hadrons E_h ~ E^{0.3–0.4} (p. 8).
- Interjet: soft gluons at angles larger than a jet's opening angle see only the jet's total colour charge, so each jet can be replaced by its parent parton (p. 85; p. 92). The whole event radiates as a coherent antenna. "Wide-angle particles do not belong to any particular jet" (p. 82, citing PETRA/PEP).
- Interjet coherence is "purely perturbative" and the string effect is non-perturbative. Both share "a respect for the colour flow topology" (p. 87).
- Depletion of the q–q̄ valley is a Lorentz-boost effect of the two colour-connected dipoles once 1/N_C² terms are dropped (Sec. 6.2.2, p. 86; Fig. 6.3 shows the string vs pQCD colour topologies, [unverified: figure not in arXiv PDF]).
- Soft limit: coherence makes the invariant density I₀ at p→0 energy independent (Eq. 3.78). Observed within ~20 % for √s = 3–130 GeV and for π, K, p from 1.6–91 GeV (p. 43, Fig. 3.16). I₀ should scale with the colour charge of the hard partons, e.g. 9/4 for gg vs qq̄ (p. 43).

**Analytical predictions (RQ2).**
- Mercedes ratios (N_C = 3), qq̄g/qq̄γ in the interquark direction: 0.44 (differential), 0.60 (projected) (Eqs. 6.6, 6.7).
- qg/qq̄ valley: 3.14 / 2.46 (Eqs. 6.8, 6.9).
- Out-of-plane/interquark ratio: 1.2 for qq̄g vs 1/4 for qq̄γ. Destructive interference makes the q–q̄ valley emptier than the out-of-plane direction (Eqs. 6.10–6.11).
- Gluon-induced flow change (Eq. 6.32) is not positive definite. The 3-jet multiplicity depends on gluon p_⊥, not on gluon energy: N_qq̄g = 2N_q(Y*₊₋) + N_g(Y*_g) (Eqs. 6.40–6.42); Lund dipole analogue in fn. 28.
- Azimuthal asymmetry (Sec. 6.4):
  - The colour-suppressed term dominates for symmetric events when Θ₊₋ ≤ 2 arctan(1/N_C) ≈ 37° (p. 97).
  - In ISR-type events A_qq̄g ≈ −(1/N_C²)A_qq̄; maximal (~2 %) at Θ₀/Θ_q ≈ 1/N_C (Eqs. 6.49–6.50, p. 98).
  - pp̄ with tagged q-jet, 70° < Θ_S < 110°, Θ₀ = 30°: full QCD A = +(4–7) % vs large-N_C/string A = −(2.5–1.5) % (p. 99, Fig. 6.8).
- Restricted-cone spectra: evolution variable Q = E_jet Θ (Eqs. 6.17–6.19).

**Confrontation with data (RQ3).**
- JADE first saw the drag effect (p. 82). Confirmed by other PETRA/PEP experiments, LEP-1, TRISTAN and Tevatron (p. 82).
- OPAL qq̄g vs qq̄γ (Z. Phys. C68 (1995) 531), interquark valley ~50°–110° (Fig. 7.1):
  - R₁₂ = 0.71 ± 0.03 (energy-ordered jets);
  - 0.60 ± 0.03 with lepton-tagged gluons;
  - 0.51 ± 0.03 after purity correction with JETSET O(α_s²) (p. 100).
  - Fit to Eqs. 6.4–6.5 plus Gaussian intrajet terms: only qualitative; the data separate qq̄γ and qq̄g more than the fit does (pp. 100–101, Fig. 7.2).
- L3 (PLB 345 (1995) 74): R_N < 1, decreasing with gluon purity, reproduced by "WIG'ged" MCs (p. 100).
- Coherent generators (HERWIG, JETSET, ARIADNE) describe the data "perfectly well" when colour flow is respected in both the perturbative and non-perturbative phases. Incoherent showers or independent fragmentation fail (p. 100).
- The p_out dependence of drag measured by L3/OPAL is "significantly weaker" than at lower energies, as pQCD expects (p. 100).
- DELPHI (Z. Phys. C70 (1996) 179), symmetric Y events, double-vertex q-tag, interval [35°, 115°]:
  - R_γ = 0.58 ± 0.06 vs pQCD 0.61 (Eqs. 7.2–7.3, Fig. 7.3). The authors: "the string effect is quantitatively explained by the perturbative prediction" (p. 101).
  - Mercedes events, minima at ±[50°, 70°]: R_g = 2.23 ± 0.37 vs pQCD ≈ 2.4 for this interval (asymptotic central-angle value 2.46, Eq. 6.9) (Eqs. 7.4–7.5, Fig. 7.5). The MLLA flow of Ref. [147] reproduces the valley depths, but the data have more particles inside the quark jets than predicted, partly because the asymptotic 1/C^g_q = 4/9 was used (p. 103).
- ALEPH (EPS 1995 contributed papers):
  - R_g ~ 2 in the highest-gluon-purity Dalitz regions; good agreement with WIG'ged MCs (p. 102).
  - Incoherent showers give R_g far too low. In some models the parton level alone nearly reproduces the data, i.e. drag is present already at the perturbative stage (p. 102).
  - No strong hadron-mass dependence (π, K, p̄) at LEP-1, as pQCD expects. However, R_g rises systematically with *decreasing* hadron mass (Fig. 7.4); the authors say string models predict decrease or no effect for the bulk of particles (p < 500 MeV) (p. 102).
  - "Jets are crooked": the soft-particle bias towards the gluon side is what the qg dipole predicts (p. 102).
  - Mean 3-jet multiplicity depends on both jet energies and angles, consistent with Eqs. 6.40/6.44 (p. 102).
- PPCA (L3, PLB 353 (1995) 145): coherent JETSET matches the data except at χ ≲ 6° (Bose–Einstein). Coherent vs incoherent JETSET differ as AO predicts (enhancement inside a cone ~Θ/√N ≈ 15°); in the coherent model the correlation almost vanishes near 25°. From this MC comparison the authors conclude that "the interference effect expected for gluons is actually visible at the hadronic level" (p. 77, Fig. 5.8).
- Tevatron:
  - CDF/D0 3-jet soft-vs-leading jet correlations show initial-to-final-state interference. D0 agrees with tree-level parton-level calculations [148,149,257] (p. 104).
  - First D0 W+jet colour-coherence data: antenna pattern analogous to qq̄g (p. 104).
  - CDF preliminary restricted-cone spectra (dijet masses 80–600 GeV, Θ = 0.168–0.466) agree with MLLA, with evolution variable E_jet Θ (p. 91).
- HERA:
  - Breit-frame current-hemisphere spectra match e⁺e⁻ at √s = Q. Peak position ξ*_p vs ln Q has slope 0.75 ± 0.05, consistent with MLLA, and no x dependence (p. 109).
  - Mean multiplicity falls short at low Q. The authors suggest the string/drag effect may partly deplete the current region (p. 108).
- Hump-backed plateau: switching AO off in JETSET gives a soft shoulder at parton level. After retuned string hadronization both versions fit the data, so the ξ spectrum alone is **not** evidence for coherence within MC models. Only continuing the cascade down to Q₀ ~ m_h discriminates (p. 42, Fig. 3.15).
- Running coupling: the spectra at √s = 3 GeV favour running over fixed α_s (p. 44). Caveat: fixed-coupling + string (JETSET) also fits PETRA/PEP (p. 44).

**Colour reconnection / interconnection.**
- Discussed only in passing, as motivation: W⁺W⁻ interconnection could shift m_W at LEP-2 (refs. [237–239], p. 83). Colour-suppressed interference also matters for perturbative rapidity-gap events in e⁺e⁻ ([240–242], p. 83).
- Gustafson's ideas for 1/N_C² terms in the Lund dipole cascade, and "negative dipoles" in ARIADNE, are cited (fn. 26, p. 83).
- No top-quark CR discussion. No quantitative reconnection results.

**Outlook (Sec. 10, pp. 124–129), colour-relevant items.**
- (4) Full 3-D flow in e⁺e⁻ 3-jet and W+jets, including energy, m_h and p_out dependence.
- (5) Onset of the perturbative regime, via soft particles perpendicular to qq̄/gg antennae in e⁺e⁻, ep and hh.
- (7) 1/N_C² azimuthal asymmetry with the gluon opposite a quasi-collinear qq̄. Also qq̄ vs qq̄g yields perpendicular to the jet axis / production plane.
- (8) Double-inclusive interjet correlations.
- (9) Use EⁿMᵐC observables rather than jet axes.
- γγ → gg at linear colliders as a clean gluon-jet source (item 2).

## Assessment
- The paper's own argument (p. 87) supports this, in our reading: the drag effect cannot settle perturbative vs non-perturbative origin. At large N_C the antenna and string predictions coincide, so agreement with Eqs. 6.6–6.9 does not exclude the string mechanism, and both coherent MCs and pQCD fit LEP-1. The discriminants offered (m_h/p_out dependence, energy evolution, 1/N_C² asymmetries) are subtle. The authors read the L3/OPAL p_out and ALEPH m_h results as favouring pQCD (pp. 100, 102), but no decisive test existed by 1996. The ALEPH mass-dependence trend (Fig. 7.4) comes from a conference contribution and is model-interpreted; check the published version.
- The quantitative agreements (R_γ, R_g) carry real uncertainties:
  - ±10 % (DELPHI R_γ) and ±17 % (R_g) experimental errors;
  - the theory side uses asymptotic large-angle formulae with unknown sub-asymptotic corrections;
  - normalisation is adjusted in Fig. 7.5;
  - the OPAL fit is only qualitative;
  - the R₁₂ values depend on gluon-purity corrections done with JETSET (model-dependent).
- The authors are proponents of the APA (Khoze is a co-author of the antenna/drag theory, ref. [30]); tone is advocacy ("persistent pressure from the APA side", p. 83). Their own caveat that the hump-backed plateau does not prove coherence once hadronization is tuned (p. 42) is an important counterweight and applies equally to interjet tests.
- Open problems they flag that remain relevant: no observation of colour-suppressed (1/N_C²) effects; the absence of these terms in MC showers (now addressed by beyond-leading-colour showers); the scale of the onset of perturbative behaviour; and LPHD's lack of a derivation. These problems feed directly into the W⁺W⁻ CR and rapidity-gap literature.
- Figures could not be inspected (placeholders in the arXiv PDF). All figure-based statements rely on text and captions. A verifier needs the journal version or the arXiv PNGs.
- For RQ1/RQ2 this is the clearest single statement of the "radiophysics" view: colour coherence is classical antenna interference that survives hadronization. It is a necessary companion to the CR/string literature.
