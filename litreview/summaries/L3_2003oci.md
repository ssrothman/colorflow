# Search for colour reconnection effects in e+e- -> W+W- -> hadrons through particle-flow studies at LEP
- **ID / links:** L3:2003oci · INSPIRE 615977 · arXiv hep-ex/0303042 · DOI 10.1016/S0370-2693(03)00490-8
- **Authors / collab, year, journal:** L3 Collaboration (P. Achard et al.), 2003, Phys. Lett. B 561 (2003) 202
- **Type:** experimental
- **Relevance:** 3 · RQs: 1b, 2, 3
- **Full text source:** zotero

Page anchors are the preprint page numbers (pp. 1–17; tables on pp. 12–13, figures on pp. 14–17).

## Takeaways
- Full LEP2 (189–209 GeV, 627 pb⁻¹) particle-flow test of inter-W colour reconnection (CR). It uses the Duchesneau method: the ratio R_N of particle flow in the intra-W interjet regions to the inter-W regions, integrated over 0.2 < φ_resc < 0.8 (Eq. 3, p. 5).
- Result: R_N = 0.915 ± 0.023 (stat) ± 0.021 (syst) (p. 7, Tab. 4). This agrees with PYTHIA without CR (0.918) and lies 4.9σ from SK-I with ~100 % reconnection (0.762) (p. 7).
- SK-I fit: best k_I = 0.08 (≈6 % reconnection probability at 189 GeV). Upper limits are k_I < 1.1 (68 % CL) and < 2.1 (95 % CL), i.e. P_reco < 45 % / 64 % at 189 GeV (p. 7). The result is also consistent with no CR.
- SK-II, ARIADNE AR2 and HERWIG cluster CR barely change R_N (ΔR_N ≤ 0.01, Tab. 4), so particle flow cannot constrain them. This model-dependence of sensitivity is the central limitation.
- The paper gives no m_W shift. It motivates CR as a bias on the 4q-channel m_W measurement (p. 2) but does not quantify that bias.

## Setup
- e+e- → W+W- → qq̄qq̄ at √s = 188.6–206.6 GeV; luminosity-weighted ⟨√s⟩ = 198.2 GeV; 626.6 pb⁻¹ (Tab. 1).
- Selection: E_vis/√s > 0.7, E⊥/E_vis < 0.2, |E∥|/E_vis < 0.2, N_cluster ≥ 40; exactly 4 Durham jets at y_cut = 0.01; lepton and ISR vetoes (p. 3). Topology cuts: the two largest interjet angles lie in 100°–140° and are non-adjacent, and the other two are < 100° (p. 3). The cuts are optimised on KORALW+PYTHIA without CR.
- Sample: 666 events (689.6 expected), efficiency 12 %, purity ≈85 %, correct W–jet pairing 91 % (Tab. 1, p. 4). The background is qq̄(γ) and ZZ in similar amounts. The semileptonic background is < 0.3 % (p. 4).
- Particles are "clusters": a non-linear combination of tracks (p_T > 100 MeV) and calorimetric clusters (E > 100 MeV) (p. 3). The angle φ is measured in four planes, each spanned by a pair of adjacent jets, and rescaled as φ_resc = j−1+φ_j,i/ψ_j,j+1 (Eq. 2, Fig. 1). The intra-W regions are A, B and the inter-W regions are C, D (Fig. 3a).
- Models compared: PYTHIA with no CR, SK-I (k_I = 0.6, 3, 1000 ⇒ P_reco ≈ 30 %, 66 %, ~100 % at 189 GeV), and SK-II (≈30 % reconnected); ARIADNE with no CR and AR2 (≈55 % of events affected); HERWIG with no CR and cluster CR (probability 1/N_c², ≈23 %) (pp. 2–3). The "probabilities" count events with any reconnection, including within the same W (p. 3).
- Cross-check: qq̄ℓν events (315 selected, 314.5 expected, 96 % purity) with the same angular cuts (pp. 5–6).

## Results
- Raw flows: data and no-CR MC agree over the full angular range in both particle and energy flow (Fig. 2). SK-I 100 % depletes the intra-W regions A, B and enhances the inter-W regions C, D (Fig. 3).
- Full sample: R_N = 0.911 ± 0.023 and R_E = 0.719 ± 0.035 (stat). Including inter-region correlations increases the statistical error by ~20 % (p. 5). R_N is 2.6× more sensitive than R_E to SK-I 100 %, so only R_N is used further (p. 5).
- R_N per energy ranges from 0.770 ± 0.086 (201.7 GeV) to 1.033 ± 0.083 (205.1 GeV) (Tab. 2). The energy dependence, attributed to different pairing purities and jet configurations, agrees with the no-CR expectation (Fig. 5, p. 5). Per the Fig. 5 caption, using the SK-I (k_I = 3) energy dependence instead of PYTHIA no-CR changes the average rescaled R_N at 189 GeV by 2.3 %.
- Final: R_N = 0.915 ± 0.023 ± 0.021 (p. 7).
- Systematics (Tab. 3; total 0.021):

  | Source | Uncertainty |
  |---|---|
  | Particle definition (energy-flow objects vs. calorimetric clusters only; dominant) | 0.016 |
  | qq̄ fragmentation (JETSET vs HERWIG) | 0.009 |
  | WW fragmentation (RMS of PYTHIA/HERWIG/ARIADNE) | 0.008 |
  | 4-jet background rate (±5 %, after a +5 % correction that raises R_N by 0.004; PYTHIA underestimates the 4-jet rate by ~10 %) | 0.004 |
  | BEC (BE32 inter-W vs intra-W only) | 0.003 |
  | ZZ background | 0.002 |

- Model predictions for R_N (Tab. 4):

  | Model | R_N |
  |---|---|
  | PYTHIA no-CR | 0.918 ± 0.003 |
  | SK-I (k_I = 0.6) | 0.896 |
  | SK-I (k_I = 3) | 0.843 |
  | SK-I 100 % | 0.762 |
  | SK-II | 0.916 |
  | ARIADNE no-CR | 0.929 |
  | AR2 | 0.919 |
  | HERWIG no-CR | 0.948 ± 0.005 |
  | HERWIG CR | 0.946 ± 0.005 |

- SK-I fit: R_N(k_I) = p₁(1−exp(−p₂k_I)) + p₃, using samples at k_I = 0, 0.6, 3, 1000. The χ² minimum is at k_I = 0.08 (≈6 % P_reco at 189 GeV). Upper limits are k_I < 1.1 (68 % CL) and 2.1 (95 % CL), i.e. P_reco < 45 % and 64 %. SK-I 100 % is disfavoured at 4.9σ (p. 7).
- Semileptonic control: the data/MC ratio of qq̄ℓν particle flow is consistent with 1 everywhere (Fig. 7b). The model-independent ratio "4q flow / (2 × qq̄ℓν flow)" agrees with no-CR, but statistics are too poor for a quantitative statement (Fig. 7c, p. 6).

## Assessment
- This is a clean, transparent observable, but its sensitivity is essentially confined to SK-I. SK-II, AR2 and HERWIG CR shift R_N by less than the total error (0.031). "No CR" therefore really means "no SK-I-type large CR", not a model-independent exclusion.
- The model-spread systematic (0.008) is small compared with the spread in absolute R_N between generators (0.918 PYTHIA vs 0.948 HERWIG, Tab. 4). That gap is ~1σ of the measurement. If HERWIG fragmentation were the truth, the inferred SK-I constraint would move noticeably. Taking the RMS rather than the full spread is generous.
- The dominant systematic, half the difference between particle definitions, is a detector-level choice. All comparisons are at detector level, so the number is not directly comparable to other experiments without each experiment's own MC normalisation. This motivated the LEP-wide normalisation to common "Cetraro" samples (see the DELPHI 2007 summary).
- The L3 limit (k_I < 2.1 at 95 % CL) is in mild tension with DELPHI's combined κ = 2.2 (+2.5 −1.3) (DELPHI:2006tie). The DELPHI combination is driven by its m_W-estimator method. DELPHI's particle-flow-only result is weak (κ = 4.13 (+21 −3.5)). In P_reco terms the tension is weaker still: DELPHI's 52 % (31–68 % at 68 % CL) is below L3's 64 % (95 % CL, at 189 GeV) limit. L3's statistical precision is better (±0.023 on R_N ≈ 0.92, vs DELPHI ±0.032 on ⟨R⟩ ≈ 0.98) even though DELPHI selects about twice as many events (1343 vs 666). This is plausibly because L3 uses all clusters, including neutrals, whereas DELPHI uses charged particles only.
- The SK-I k_I convention here gives a default of 0.6 ⇒ 30 % at 189 GeV. DELPHI quotes 0.66 as the default. The κ-to-P_reco map depends on √s, so cross-paper comparisons should use P_reco at a stated energy.
